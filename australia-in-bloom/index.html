FLOWERS, NURSERY PLANTS & TURF

Australia in bloom.

Behind a bouquet or a backyard garden is a nationwide growing industry. Follow where its value comes from, which states grow it, and how Australia’s flower trade is changing.

This story focuses on the handbook’s “other horticulture” group: nursery plants, cut flowers and turf. Fruit, vegetables and nuts are outside this comparison.

01

THE INDUSTRY

The biggest business is living plants.

Nurseries generated 81.5% of this group’s production value in 2024/25. Cut flowers are a smaller industry, but their value recovered more strongly over the latest year.

A RECOVERY WITH DIFFERENT SPEEDS

Flowers grew 12.0% in the latest year.

Nursery production value rose 4.1%. Turf was almost unchanged, down 0.2%. The green cells reveal the recovery after two weaker years for flowers and nurseries.

02

THE GROWING REGIONS

Where flowers grow changes the picture.

Victoria leads in total flower production value. Relative to population, Western Australia leads the six states with reported figures. These are measures of production, not household flower spending.

GROWING AND EXPORTING ARE DIFFERENT ROLES

Queensland accounts for 58.9% of flower export value.

Its share of production value is 19.0%. These percentages have different national totals as their denominators. They do not mean that 58.9% of Queensland’s flowers are exported.

03

THE FLOWER TRADE

A bouquet can have global roots.

Australia imported $108.1 million in cut flowers in 2024/25. Malaysia remained the largest named source. Vietnam’s value nearly doubled in one year and moved from fifth to third among the five named origins.

A SMALLER OUTWARD TRADE

Japan, the Netherlands and the US received 70.4% of flower export value.

Australia’s cut flower exports were valued at $8.0m in 2024/25. The leading destinations are concentrated, even though the overall outward trade is much smaller than imports.

04

WHAT IS CHANGING

Higher value tells only part of the story.

Flower imports exceed exports in every year shown. Nurseries, meanwhile, recovered in both value and units in 2024/25, but remained below their 2020/21 level. Dollar growth alone does not show that more plants were grown.

THREE THINGS TO TAKE AWAY

Plants set the scale. Nurseries dominate the value of this part of horticulture; flowers experienced the strongest latest-year recovery.

Regions play different roles. Victoria leads flower production, WA leads production per resident, and Queensland leads flower exports.

The trade mix is shifting. Vietnam’s growing role accounts for most of the latest increase in flower import value. The data does not establish why.

ABOUT THE DATA

Sources, measures and limits

Industry and trade

Hort Innovation, Australian Horticulture Statistics Handbook 2024/25, “All Other Horticulture”, produced by Freshlogic by Kynetec. Printed pages 406, 409–411, 413–415 and 419. Trade figures are credited to Global Trade Atlas in the handbook.

The 2024/25 edition is the latest handbook located as at 29 September 2026. Five-year comparisons use that edition’s series, rather than mixing vintages.

Population and geography

Australian Bureau of Statistics, National, state and territory population, March 2026: Table 4 (310104.xlsx), persons at 30 June 2025, matched to the production year. This is a separate statistical source.

Natural Earth public-domain state and country boundaries. Australian maps use an Albers-style equal-area conic projection; the world map uses Equal Earth. Small offshore territories are omitted from the regional map.

Production value describes the value of output at the producer level. Wholesale supply value is a different supply-chain measure: the handbook reports $452.7m for cut flowers in 2024/25. It should not be added to production or interpreted as retail spending.

All dollars are nominal Australian dollars. Values are estimates, mostly rounded to A$0.1m. State totals may not add exactly to the national total. Missing state values and values reported as “<1” are preserved; they are not converted to zero or invented precise amounts. Flower production volumes and variety-level trends are not available in the selected handbook tables, so this story compares growing regions.

Method: per-resident production = state production in A$m × 1,000,000 ÷ ABS residents. Indexed value = annual value ÷ 2020/21 value × 100. Annual growth = (current value ÷ previous value − 1) × 100. The visualisation joins Hort Innovation and ABS by state and the matching June 2025 reference period.
